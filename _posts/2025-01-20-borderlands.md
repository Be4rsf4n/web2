# Borderlands Movie Review: A Video Game Adaptation Years in the Making

The long-awaited Borderlands movie has finally hit theaters, though perhaps not with the impact fans were hoping for. As a film adaptation of the beloved video game series known primarily for its gameplay rather than its story, the movie faced an uphill battle from the start. After speaking with viewers and analyzing the final product, it's clear why this $100+ million project spent years sitting on studio shelves before its release.

## A Star-Studded Cast Meets Development Hell

One of the most striking aspects of the Borderlands film is its impressive ensemble cast, featuring Oscar winners Cate Blanchett and Jamie Lee Curtis, alongside comedy stars Kevin Hart and Jack Black. However, this star power comes with an interesting backstory - the movie was apparently completed years ago and shelved, only recently getting its theatrical release. This explains why some of the casting choices feel somewhat dated, with Jamie Lee Curtis appearing pre-"Everything Everywhere All at Once" fame and in her "Halloween sequel era."

The reported $100+ million budget seems to have gone primarily to securing this high-profile talent, though some questionable casting decisions raised eyebrows among fans. Most notably, Jack Black's casting as a robot character sparked debate about why the original game's voice actor wasn't used, given that the role required no physical acting.

## Where The Film Stumbles

The movie's primary issues stem from its screenplay, which feels underdeveloped and unpolished. Dialogue between characters often comes across as unnatural, with emotional scenes particularly suffering from awkward writing. One viewer noted that even dramatic moments, such as a confrontation between Blanchett's character Lilith and her mother figure (played by Jamie Lee Curtis), fall flat due to stilted dialogue that "nobody would actually say."

The film's attempts at humor also miss their mark more often than not. A particularly notorious scene features Jack Black's robot character spending nearly a full minute "pooping out" bullet casings after a battle, complete with sound effects and reaction shots. The scene's extended runtime and commitment to the bit left theater audiences in uncomfortable silence rather than laughter.

## A Third Act Salvation?

Despite its early struggles, the film's final third manages to pull together somewhat more coherently. While the plot twists remain predictable, the concluding action sequences and narrative wrap-up provide more engaging entertainment than the preceding acts. This improvement in the final stretch suggests that somewhere within this troubled production, there might have been potential for a more compelling video game adaptation.

## The Fan Perspective

Opening night audiences included die-hard Borderlands fans who seemed more receptive to the film's attempts at fan service and game-specific humor. However, even among this presumably favorable crowd, reactions were mixed. The biggest audience reaction reportedly came from an unexpected Bobby Lee cameo rather than any of the film's intended set pieces or emotional moments.

## The Verdict

The Borderlands movie serves as yet another example of the challenges faced when adapting video games to film, particularly when the source material is more celebrated for its gameplay than its narrative. Despite its impressive cast and considerable budget, the final product struggles to capture what made the games special while also failing to stand on its own as an engaging sci-fi action film.

For those unfamiliar with the game series, the movie might prove slightly more palatable than its 0% Rotten Tomatoes score suggests. However, for fans hoping to see their favorite shooter translated successfully to the big screen, this long-delayed adaptation may feel like a missed opportunity.
