Alright! This is a new test post for Bullet Train. 

![Alt text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Bullet_Train_Logo.svg)

One of my favorite films, this was the first film to put director David Leitch on my radar. Right away, the movie establishes itself as highly stylized. The character graphics intros (always love when a movie has the confidence to go cartoonish) compare well to the evil exes from Scott Pilgrim, hinting at the lunacy to follow. Wardrobe, fight choreo, and shot pacing all synchronize to augment the rapid escalation of stakes throughout the movie. There's a really fun conceit with continual stops along the train route, which provide a great device to bookend scenes. Plus, with the separate train cars, Leitch is really able to isolate scenes in both space and time without having to spend a lot of overhead on sending irrelevant characters off screen.

This lets him get to his calling card: bleeding edge fight choreography. The compartments offer the perfect space for high speed CQC and prop utilization with storage, luggage, collateral passengers, and the briefcase. My personal fight choreo comp is always going to fall back against South Asian film (borderline unbeatable unless Bruce Lee or Jackie Chan or the guy from Wu Warriors enter the chat), and the scenes here hold their own on a pure technical level. The integration with character flashbacks, punchlines, and maximal utilization of the space with fight scenes gradually expanding to involve the entire train and the outside environment are unmatched for a live-action movie. At least to me. Maybe there's homage or plagiarism I'm missing to someone else's prior work, but otherwise this stuff is unbelievably imaginative.

Casting is also just phenomenal. I feel like any of the projects involving the big shots - Clooney, Pitt, Sandler (yes, Sandler) - risk letting them cook and place everybody else as an expendable. Involving strong character exposition for Joey King, Aaron Taylor Johnson and Bryan Tyree Henry realy helped offset that, so there's at least a reasonable 4 piece ensemble. The character motivations and progression are very well-balanced, so sure, you want Brad Pitt to end up with the briefcase, but you're reasonably ok with any of them walking away with it. Beyond the core, though, the side character pulls had me so excited as I saw them. Establishing Hiroyuki Sanada (friggin Kenji from Rush Hour 3) with a samurai sword just put me back in 4th grade; I was ready to suspend all disbelief for the plot if it ended in a swordfight with this man. But then the side assassin character features for Bad Bunny and Zazie Beetz with the slow roll storytelling for their connection was just chef's kiss. For all the Agatha Christie work that's been redone to death for theaters, the plot on this felt like the most honest spiritual successor to "And Then There Were None". But then again, I haven't seen most of those adaptations - and I wish I could forget Death on the Nile. Update based on Wikipedia: apparently the film's based on Maria Beetle, a 2010 mystery novel by Kotaro Isaka. I guess keep an eye out for more adaptations of his work.

What else? I think I need to watch again to see if the soundtrack held up. I remember watching in theaters and it seemed like audio mixing for the scenes was mostly enough to make it memorable. But yeah don't think a lot of the music really held up. Oh, and Sony's really doing well carving out a role for themselves as industry leaders for CGI integration in both a technical and stylistic sense. Sure, there's probably some crosstalk from their experience with Playstation, but there's a judiciousness in when and how to deploy the technology that blur the lines very well. Additionally, there's a track record now between this and Spider-Verse for Sony movies to execute scenes in ways I have never seen. After a decade of studios working to bring comics to reality, it feels like Sony is setting the stage for converting reality into fantasy.

Overall, absolutely love Bullet Train. Would not change a thing about it, would and do watch it every time I hear somebody hasn't seen it or if there's just been a shortage of high octane movies. Can watch it with pretty much all age groups.
